  • Tesco, Národní 26 · 10 min · the largest in the center, long hours. Map

  • Pharmacy: Dr.Max, Ječná 1 · 4 min · Mon–Fri 8–16:30. Dr.Max, Karlovo náměstí 8 · 7 min · Mon–Fri 7–19. 24 hours: Lékárna Palackého, Palackého 5 · 7 min (ring at the night window). Dental emergency in the same building. Map
  • Hospital emergency: VFN (General University Hospital), U Nemocnice 2 · 7 min · 24/7. Emergency number 112, English spoken. Map
  • ATMs: bank ATMs only (ČSOB, Česká spořitelna, Komerční banka, all around Karlovo náměstí). Yellow Euronet machines cost up to 12% more. Whatever the machine, decline "conversion" and take crowns.
  • Cash exchange: rarely needed; cards work almost everywhere. If you must: eXchange, Kaprova 14 in the Old Town. Never the booths on Wenceslas Square.
  • Post office: Jindřišská 14 · 12 min · seven days.
  • Luggage: self-service lockers at Praha hlavní nádraží (main station), lower hall, from 80 CZK/day, 18 min. Or app-based storage (Radical Storage, Bounce), around 5 EUR per bag per day.
  • Laundry: the studio has a washer and dryer.
  • SIM / eSIM: Vodafone, O2 and T-Mobile shops on Wenceslas Square; an eSIM (Airalo or similar) is simpler.

7 · Practical Prague

  • Money: crowns (CZK), not euros. Cards and phone payments everywhere. Tip about 10% at table service; say the total before they run the card, the terminals have a tip step.
  • Tap water is good. Ask for "kohoutková" (tap) in restaurants.
  • Shops open on Sundays. Supermarkets close on about eight public holidays a year and at noon on 24 December.
  • Quiet hours are 22:00–6:00 by law and custom, and this is a residential house with a shared courtyard.
  • Taxis: Bolt, Uber or Liftago. Never wave one down in the center.
  • Smoking is banned inside bars and restaurants.
  • Emergencies: 112. Police 158, ambulance 155, fire 150.
  • Museums are mostly closed on Mondays.

8 · Getting around

  • Metro: B at Karlovo náměstí (4 min), C at I. P. Pavlova (9 min), A and C at Muzeum (10 min). Runs 5:00–24:00.

    Every tram has an orange terminal at the middle doors: tap a bank card, take the printed ticket, done. Paper tickets from yellow machines must be stamped once in the yellow validator. Inspectors are real and fine on the spot.
  • Airport: metro A to Nádraží Veleslavín, then trolleybus 59 to the terminals. About 45 minutes; one 90-minute ticket covers the whole trip. Bolt or Uber: 25–35 minutes, roughly 600–900 CZK.
  • Trains: Praha hlavní nádraží (main station) is 18 minutes on foot or one metro stop from Muzeum. Vienna 4 h, Berlin 4 h, Dresden 2 h 15.
  • Walking beats everything inside the center: Old Town Square 20 minutes, Charles Bridge 25.

9 · A few genuinely useful tips

  • New Town Hall tower on Karlovo náměstí (4 min): the closest good view of the city for a few crowns. Tue–Sun 10–18, spring to mid-December. The 1419 defenestration happened here.
  • Prague Castle: courtyards and the cathedral entrance are free; interiors need a ticket. Go before 9:00 or after 16:00. Tram 22 from Štěpánská gets you there in 20 minutes.
  • Charles Bridge at 7:00 is a different bridge from Charles Bridge at 14:00.
  • Kino Světozor (Vodičkova 41, 8 min) screens art-house and Czech films with English subtitles; look for "English friendly" in the program.
  • Dvořák Museum, Villa Amerika, Ke Karlovu 20 (8 min): a small baroque summer palace with his piano and manuscripts. The building is heading into a long renovation, so check it is open before walking over.
  • Botanical Garden of Charles University, Na Slupi 16 (13 min): quiet greenhouses and a hillside garden nobody visits.
  • Concerts: the Rudolfinum and the Municipal House are the real halls; the "Vivaldi in a church" concerts sold on the street are fine but pricey for what they are.
  • Sunday: Náplavka is quiet, Vyšehrad is calm, Vinohrady's cafés are full of locals. Go where they go.

10 · This house and the neighborhood

The house at the White Lion, Žitná 14, was Antonín Dvořák's home from 1877 until his death in 1904. He wrote most of his mature work here, including the Slavonic Dances and the opera Rusalka, and asked the landlord never to rent to anyone who owned a piano, so that he could work in silence. The courtyard is still quiet. The bronze bust above the entrance, by Otakar Španiel, was placed in 1927.

Nové Město, the New Town, was "new" in 1348, when Charles IV laid it out around three market squares; Karlovo náměstí, four minutes away, was the largest square in medieval Europe. The blocks between Karlovo náměstí and Wenceslas Square are where Prague works, studies and eats lunch, which is why the good places here are cheaper and calmer than the same thing ten minutes north.

Places we would skip

  • Restaurants and exchange booths on Wenceslas Square and Karlova street; the same food costs half at U Šumavy.
  • Trdelník stands. Sweet, photogenic, not Czech. Have a větrník at Myšák instead.
  • Euronet ATMs, and any ATM's offer to "convert."
  • "Traditional Czech" pubs with waiters outside waving menus. A real Czech pub has no one outside.

Day trips

Five places worth leaving Prague for, by public transport, with honest timings. The main station (Hlavní nádraží) is 18 minutes on foot or one metro stop from Muzeum. Buy train tickets at cd.cz or in the ČD app; buses at regiojet.com. Prices are for booking a few days ahead.

Kutná Hora

1 h by train from Hlavní nádraží to Kutná Hora hl. n., roughly hourly, about 150–250 CZK return. The ossuary is a 15-minute walk from that station; the old town is one more stop on the tiny local train.

The silver mines that paid for Charles IV's Prague. Two things: the Sedlec Ossuary, a chapel decorated with the bones of 40,000 people, and St Barbara's Cathedral, a Gothic church as good as St Vitus with a tenth of the visitors. Between them, a handsome small town for lunch.

Know before you go: the ossuary now sells only timed tickets, bought online in advance (sedlec.info), about 90 CZK alone or 360 CZK combined with the cathedral; photography inside is banned. Half a day is enough; a full day if you walk the old town properly.

Karlštejn

40 min by train from Hlavní nádraží (also from Smíchov), hourly, about 60–80 CZK each way, then a 25-minute uphill walk through the village.

Charles IV built it in the 1350s to hold the imperial crown jewels, and it still looks like the castle a child would draw. Interiors are by guided tour only; the standard "Emperor's Residence" tour is about 300 CZK, and the Chapel of the Holy Cross tour is the one worth booking ahead in summer. Closed Mondays and most of winter; check hrad-karlstejn.cz.

Know before you go: the village street up to the gate is a gauntlet of souvenir stalls; eat in Prague. The walk along the Berounka river back toward Beroun is lovely if you have an afternoon.

Český Krumlov

2 h 40 by RegioJet bus from Na Knížecí (metro B to Anděl), hourly, 250–380 CZK each way; book a seat. Trains take longer and change.

A UNESCO town bent around a loop of the Vltava under a giant castle. In the middle of the day it is one of the most visited places in the country; by 18:00 the day-trippers have gone and it is one of the most beautiful. Climb the castle tower, walk the Baroque theatre if the tour is running, rent a canoe in summer.

Honest advice: as a day trip it is six hours on a bus for four in the town. If you can, make it one night; Krumlov after dark is the reason to go.

Plzeň

1 h 30 by train from Hlavní nádraží, hourly, about 150–250 CZK each way. The brewery is a ten-minute walk downhill from the station.

Where lager was invented in 1842. The Pilsner Urquell brewery tour (about 100 minutes, English tours several times a day, 430 CZK, book at prazdrojvisit.cz) ends in the cellars with unfiltered beer straight from the barrel, which is the reason to come. The main square with the cathedral tower and the Brewery Museum fill the rest of an afternoon.

Know before you go: the 14:00-ish English tour lets you leave Prague after breakfast and be home for dinner.

Dresden

2 h 15 by EuroCity train from Hlavní nádraží, every two hours, from about 10–20 EUR each way booked ahead (cd.cz or bahn.de). Bring your passport or ID card.

The train follows the Elbe through the sandstone gorge, which is half the trip. In Dresden: the rebuilt Frauenkirche, the Zwinger and Old Masters gallery, the Brühl terrace along the river, and the Green Vault if you booked. Back by 21:00.

Know before you go: the Old Town is compact; four hours is enough. Cash is more useful in Germany than in Prague.

Terezín

1 h by bus from Praha, Nádraží Holešovice (metro C), roughly hourly from platform 7, about 60–100 CZK each way; the bus stops at the Small Fortress and in the town.

The Habsburg garrison town the Nazis turned into a ghetto and transit camp. The memorial covers the Small Fortress, the Ghetto Museum and the Magdeburg barracks (pamatnik-terezin.cz). Go independently rather than with a coach tour, and give it the whole day. Not a place for a rushed afternoon.

Also possible

  • Bohemian Switzerland (Pravčická brána): 2 h by train to Děčín and bus; a full day of hiking, best May–October.
  • Vienna or Berlin: 4 h by train each way, so an overnight, not a day.
  • Konopiště (Franz Ferdinand's hunting castle): 1 h by train to Benešov; a quieter alternative to Karlštejn.
